# Stellar hybrid emblem v1

This production candidate follows the approved hybrid route for [#176](https://github.com/DanielssonThomas/Stellar/issues/176). The experimental [#251](https://github.com/DanielssonThomas/Stellar/issues/251) manifest uses 81 logical planes and 91 runtime image files in `layers/`, including 24 strand-shaped hair fragments and their two original assembled sources. Manifest paint order defines the canonical emblem. `previews/canonical.png` is their transparent 1280 × 1280 assembly. The earlier study and original concept sheet remain in `../assets/`; they are provenance, not runtime dependencies.

## Experimental fragmentation (#251)

The optional `rasterSizes` manifest field advertises 128, 256, 512, 768 and 1024-pixel versions of every PNG fragment and assembled source, under `resolutions/<width>/<original-file>`. All variants retain the full registered canvas and transparency; 1280-pixel originals and canonical previews remain unchanged. The molecule waits for its first container measurement before inserting responsive images, then updates `sizes` with a `ResizeObserver`. Width-descriptor `srcset` lets the browser account for device pixel ratio, including display/zoom changes, for visible fragments and hidden assembled-source preloads. SVG rings remain vector; older manifests without size metadata keep their original loading behavior. A browser may retain a larger cached candidate after the container shrinks.

Run `node libs/stellar-emblem/render-production.mjs` to regenerate and validate the complete pack, or `node libs/stellar-emblem/render-resolutions.mjs` to regenerate just resolution variants from the registered originals. Run `node libs/stellar-emblem/sync-runtime.mjs` afterward to copy all referenced assets into the desktop public pack and refresh its verified SHA-256 inventory. Resolution export uses Canvas high-quality resampling directly from each original; downsampled variants are intentionally not pixel-identical to their full-resolution sources. Settled runtime comparison uses independently deduplicated originals with the same resolution selection.

At a 480 CSS-pixel container, cold-load Chromium checks select 512-pixel PNGs at 1× density, 1024-pixel PNGs at 2×, and the original 1280-pixel PNGs at 3× without fetching other PNG tiers. The 512-pixel tier is about 1.35 MB, compared with 6.49 MB for the original tier. All six tiers together occupy about 15.00 MB; only the selected assets are requested. Each tier retains the 8 MiB runtime budget, while the embedded distribution has a separate 16 MiB budget. `previews/validation.json` records exact byte counts and theoretical uncompressed RGBA sizes, not measured browser/GPU allocation. This is a loading optimization; native frame-time improvement still requires measurement.

Each hair image is partitioned into twelve disjoint curved lock groups using the built-in ImageGen-authored `sources/*-hair-segmentation-v2.png` maps. The maps label strand boundaries rather than repainting hair. `render-strands.mjs` assigns every original pixel to one label, extends labels over small map/silhouette differences, exports transparent fragments and verifies their recomposition byte-for-byte. These are approximate strand groups, not individually repainted hairs. The gold ornament uses fourteen leaf regions plus its intact gem/setting; violet uses six leaves plus its gem/setting. Their original pixels are extracted with the same segmentation process and restored through complete assembly groups. Largest interior cores guide label growth through each real ornament, avoiding stray outlines on other leaves. Body images use eleven curved organic regions per color, authored with ImageGen body segmentation maps and extracted from the original pixels. No region retains more than 22% of its visible source silhouette. All gold body pieces use explicit positive source depths from 530 to 830, below their own hair. Their indexed slots remain layout bookkeeping only; no gold character fragment occupies the negative-depth violet side. Violet body, hair and ornament pieces remain at negative depth. The exporter rejects negligible fragments and verifies isolated source recomposition plus the complete rasterized-source registration. Original raster files and assembled previews remain unchanged. Settlement uses the validated complete `assembly` group to restore each full original body/hair/ornament image and secondary ring SVG, avoiding seams at dock sizes; originals preload while idle. Invalid or incomplete group metadata fails validation.

Fragments use a scrambled depth order within their own hair or ornament group, lateral offsets and small rotations in the exploded pose. All body fragments remain behind their hair and all ornaments in front. All five rings share a straight center axis with zero lateral offset; the complete starred outer frame remains centered at depth zero. The optional `outer-dense` profile uses 49.4-source-unit nominal spacing with continuously smaller gaps toward both ends. The main starred ring stays whole. Each of the four secondary circles has three 120-degree pixel-partitioned arcs. They remain coaxial but use uneven explicit source depths of [-570, -480, -410], [-330, -240, -170], [150, 240, 330] and [390, 480, 570]. A thirteen-plane core at spacing 80 preserves the previous raster fragment depths and overall extent; explicit per-arc depths override indexed depth. Raster fragment centers move inward according to an elliptical radial taper of 0.94, using their measured alpha-weighted centroids after exploded rotation/scatter. The contraction increases with absolute depth; individual locks keep their shape and size. Ring planes have no centroid and stay on the axis. The camera, one-second transitions, in-plane ring motion and reduced-motion behavior remain. This is an experimental alternative to the historical uniformly spaced fifteen-plane presentation described below, pending user design review. Browser evidence does not qualify native performance.

## Artwork and registration

The four secondary rings alternate counter-clockwise, clockwise, counter-clockwise and clockwise from `inner-circle` through `inner-circle-4`; each ring's three arcs share its direction. The whole starred main ring keeps its clockwise turn. This changes only animation metadata. Ring depths, geometry, fragmentation and source pixels remain identical. The optional music visualizer adds temporary arc kicks and brightness changes in its own animation wrapper, then restores the exact rest pose for assembly.

The darker violet profile faces left behind the gold profile facing right. The historical fifteen-plane baseline reused one body image through three registered forehead/nose/lower-face clips, plus one coherent hair image and one botanical/gemstone pin. The purple pin uses silver/lavender leaves; the gold pin retains its purple gemstone. No dangling earrings are present. Five complete SVG circle planes complete the fifteen logical pieces. Four cardinal stars are composed into the outer frame so they rotate with it. The three additional inner circles exactly share the original inner-circle geometry and overlap at zero pose. The six large raster groups preserve flowing hair and modeled faces without pretending that a full master or arbitrary crops are independent layers.

`sources/master-guide-v1.png` is the untouched built-in imagegen visual guide, derived from the supplied concept. Six further built-in imagegen edits produced the untouched `sources/*-generated.png` cutouts. No API-key fallback or paid external route was used. The generator returned 1254 × 1254 PNGs, despite requesting a 1280 square canvas. It also drifted in placement. `registration.json` records the exact uniform scale and translation used to place each complete transparent source into the final 1280 square export canvas. The export does no repainting, background removal, clipping, filtering or artificial splitting. All creative extraction/refinement was done by imagegen. Prompt specifications are preserved in `sources/generation-prompts.md`.

This registered assembly is a deliberate new canonical reconstruction, not a pixel-identical segmentation of the guide. Faces sit slightly lower, hair waves and pins differ, and the geometry is cleaner than the guide. The gold/violet identity and opposite profiles remain. The smaller full circle is geometrically complete even where hair overlaps it in the foreground. SVG source is editable; raster source cutouts and registration are retained for reproducible exports. Future local strand edits would require an image-editing source tool, rather than claiming these PNGs are vector paths.

All exported layers share `viewBox: [0, 0, 1280, 1280]`, dimensions, origin and registration. Every assembled translation and rotation is zero. Paint-order `z` is metadata; the consumer adds no depth in the assembled pose. Explicit `planeIndex` metadata defines independent depth order. The application owns the emblem's container position and size separately.

## Reproduction and validation

The #202 correction introduced the oblique separated scene. #204 retains all six registered raster bytes and zero-pose registration, composes the existing star geometry into the outer frame, adds three matching inner-circle planes and uses uniform source-unit spacing. Rings now rotate only in plane; there is no out-of-plane precession. The common upper-left camera and responsive fitting live in the Angular molecule/Stage, not in these image files. See the [preserved correction references](../fixtures/idle-correction/README.md). The flat Canvas preview does not render the 3D camera; use the actual component/native captures to assess the separated idle presentation.

From the installed workspace, run `node libs/stellar-emblem/render-production.mjs`. It exports the complete registered PNG layers from untouched sources, generates the five safe geometric SVGs and manifest, then recreates the canonical and 512/48/64/96 px transparent previews and dark contact sheet. Generated outputs are deterministic for the same Chromium renderer. Canvas drawing is standard composition/export, not a second creative rendering route.

The script checks source dimensions, real transparent/opaque/partial alpha, empty export borders, safe SVG element/attribute and local-reference allowlists, safe relative layer paths and zero assembly poses. It compares direct canonical composition against applying the manifest's assembled poses. `previews/validation.json` records the actual runtime byte count and an 8 MiB per-tier runtime budget for the manifest, fragments and assembled originals (6,492,501 bytes at full resolution). Sources/history and review previews are excluded from this runtime budget. Runtime SVGs contain no embedded rasters, scripts, event handlers, external references or foreign objects. The consumer validates safe SVG/PNG/WebP paths and retains its existing image load-failure fallback.

The inspected contact sheet preserves a readable gold foreground/violet background, two profile edges, both rings and cardinal accents at 512 px. At 96 px the hair groups and contrasting pins remain visible; at 64 px the silhouette and colour separation are stronger than the individual ornaments. At 48 px the pin facets and individual strands merge; the opposite profiles and gold frame remain recognizable. This is an explicit scale tradeoff, not a claim that every full-size detail survives docking. `previews/exploded512.png` is a flat registration preview with the depth camera omitted; it does not represent the separated idle scene.

Visual acceptance is recorded on the issue after Codex and an independent image-capable reviewer inspect these artifacts. Text-only Foreman output is not represented as visual inspection. This pack does not qualify native performance or autonomous workers.

The original #176 independent image-capable review accepted the visual direction, then-twelve meaningful groups and documented dock-size tradeoff. The #204 geometry/motion refinement has separate rendered component and native verification. A suspected matte-fringe defect was retracted after inspecting actual Chromium alpha composition. `previews/before-edge-review.png` and `before-edge-review-enlarged.png` preserve that evidence on dark and checkerboard backgrounds. The enlarged inspection displays untouched 180 × 150 source regions over those backgrounds; no alpha editing or corrective generation was applied. For example, the violet hair's isolated blue pixel at source `(413, 45)` has RGBA `(0, 0, 255, 1)`, only 1/255 opacity. Raw image previews can exaggerate this low-alpha RGB; normal browser composition preserves the intended clean edge. Review the composited previews when assessing actual displayed appearance.

## Registered face fragmentation (#211)

The #221 candidate keeps both ornaments in front of their own hair throughout assembly. The violet hair also stays in front of every violet body fragment: its previous depth index put it behind the body during motion even though its final paint order was in front, making covered strands suddenly appear near settlement. The exporter rejects either reversed relationship. Depth spacing, camera and durations are unchanged.

The violet body uses scale 0.72 at (37, 131), reducing its size by 10% from the earlier 0.8 registration and placing its profile further left and down. At alpha 24/255 or greater, its maximum radius is 492.13 source units and the untouched violet hair's is 490.86. Both clear the outer frame's inner edge at radius 497.5; the exporter requires at least two source units of clearance. This prevents the purple contour from running under the outer gold stroke without clipping or repainting it. The gold body remains raised by 30 units and moved right by 7 to join the existing hair contour. The two body exports and composed previews change; the six original generated sources and all other exported images retain their original pixels. Every assembled transform remains zero. Native evidence and comparison with the supplied canonical reference determine visual acceptance, not the zero-pose invariant alone.

The emblem molecule also accepts an explicit `solActive` presentation input. Once assembly actually settles, it reveals a single red circle concentric with the existing outer gold circle and two narrow eye glints; the effect scales with the emblem, fades on removal and respects reduced motion. The app binds this input to the restored persistent voice composition model, including explicit Sol drafting and confirmed reset to Terra. This input communicates selected intent and does not itself verify the actual inference provider or model.

The unchanged body images are partitioned at source rows512 and640 into three disjoint full-width regions. No independent idle plane retains a whole face. Optional validated integer clip rectangles preserve backwards compatibility for older unclipped manifests. All fifteen planes use equal240-source-unit depth spacing, while the camera, ring rotation and1000ms durations remain unchanged.

The exporter verifies the clipped full-resolution composition byte-for-byte against the original unclipped image stack. Once real camera/layer animations settle, only complete disjoint partitions with zero assembled poses, matching origins and contiguous paint order consolidate to one original image; this avoids fractional clip-edge antialias seams at smaller sizes. Interrupted and return motion restores the three clips. Browser pixel comparisons at supported large/small viewports verify exact settled composition against an independently deduplicated original image stack. Raster/SVG files and canonical1280/512/48/64/96 previews retain their original bytes. The flat layer contact sheet displays the actual clipped regions.

The source exporter verifies every segmented body/hair/ornament and each ring's three arcs against its isolated rasterized source, then checks the full fragment composition against independently rasterized originals. Direct vector-SVG Canvas composition can round alpha differently from rasterized arc composition, so validation records fragmentAssemblyPixelIdentical=false and rasterizedRegistrationPixelIdentical=true. Canonical and dock previews use the untouched original sources and retain their bytes. The component restores original SVGs only after settlement; browser pixel comparisons verify exact settled assembly against an independent original-image stack at large and small sizes.

Generated fragment PNGs use lossless level-9 recompression of the original filtered scanlines, reducing encoded size without decoding or modifying pixels. Original source PNGs are untouched. Run corepack pnpm exec vitest run --config libs/stellar-emblem/vitest.config.mjs for the compression integrity tests.
